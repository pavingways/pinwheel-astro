"What does an app cost?" is the first question in almost every initial conversation – and every honest answer starts with: **it depends.** That is not an evasion. An app is not an off-the-shelf product but software whose effort depends on concrete, nameable factors. Those factors are exactly what we break down here – so you can put quotes into perspective and plan your budget realistically.

## The five big cost drivers

### 1. Scope of functionality

The most obvious factor. An app that displays content and receives push notifications is a different project from a customer portal with login, contracts and payment features. Rule of thumb: it is not the number of screens that drives cost, but the number of **states and edge cases** – what happens offline, what happens when a session expires, what happens when a payment fails?

### 2. Interfaces and backend

Almost every commercially relevant app talks to systems behind the scenes: CMS, shop, CRM, internal IT. Where clean APIs exist, this goes quickly. Where interfaces first have to be designed, coordinated and built, it often becomes a subproject of its own – not infrequently half of the total effort.

### 3. Design ambition

Standard system components are inexpensive and feel familiar. A fully custom design with animations and its own design language costs more – in design **and** development. Either can be the right call; it should just be a conscious decision.

### 4. Platform strategy

Developing iOS and Android natively as separate apps doubles large parts of the effort. [Cross-platform development](/en/technologies/) with Ionic/Capacitor, React Native or Flutter delivers both platforms from one codebase – for most requirements the more economical choice, and the reason we have relied on it for years.

### 5. Quality assurance and operations

Testing, automated pipelines, app-store processes: this part tends to get lowballed in quotes – and takes its revenge in operations. Automation costs a little up front and saves money on every single release afterwards.

## Ballpark figures – with all due caution

Every serious number needs this caveat: **without a requirements analysis, every number is a guess.** As a rough orientation for the league a project typically plays in:

- **Prototype / click dummy** – the cheapest way to make an idea testable before serious budget flows: from a few thousand Swiss francs.
- **A focused app with standard features** (content, push, a simple integration): usually in the low to mid five-figure range.
- **A business-critical app with backend, login and integrations** (customer portal, companion app to your product): mid five-figure to six-figure range.
- **Enterprise projects** with compliance requirements, multiple systems and a long lifecycle: individual – here integration and organisation dominate, not the app's surface.

More important than the initial number: **calculated over five years, half of the total cost often falls on the time after launch.** Every year brings new OS versions, new devices, new store guidelines and security updates. If you plan for that from the start – for example with [app maintenance at a fixed monthly price](/en/app-maintenance/) – there are no surprises.

## How to lower the cost without sacrificing quality

1. **Requirements first.** A clean requirements analysis costs little and prevents the most expensive kind of effort: building the wrong thing. That is why [every one of our projects](/en/app-development/) starts there.
2. **MVP instead of wish list.** Solve the core problem first, then extend based on real user feedback.
3. **Hybrid and cross-platform where it fits.** One codebase, both stores. Hybrid means going native wherever it makes sense.
4. **Automate.** CI/CD pipelines make releases cheap and reproducible – for years.
5. **Use what exists.** Good APIs, a maintained design system or an experienced in-house team measurably reduce the effort.
